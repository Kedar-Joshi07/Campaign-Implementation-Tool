# Client explanation and operator checklist

Suggested narrative: “We learn demographic profiles from verified historical buyers of the selected products. We score a separate prospect population for resemblance to those profiles, apply your strict audience criteria, and select people meeting the chosen similarity minimum. The result can be reviewed and exported through the relevant contact channel.”

A score of 90 means the weighted distance to the nearest supported learned buyer profile is at most .10 under the declared metric. It does not mean 90% purchase probability, accuracy, confidence, percentile, or that 90% of historical buyers match. Prefer “90/100 index” or “90+”. This is a method-specific descriptive index.

The purchase-probability capability remains implemented: existing models, calibration, thresholds, feedback and historical results. Similarity has a separate readiness path. It does not fix the current weak predictive evidence or historical campaign-split problem.

The 125K customers and 5M prospects are independent populations. Historical customers link to their sales through customer_id; new prospects use compatible features. Category/date/unit alignment and distribution comparisons matter; matching their IDs is unnecessary.

Some products are unavailable because catalog presence is not buyer evidence. At the inspected SHA, sales history covers 36 of the 48 catalog products. This method also needs enough distinct relevant buyers and fit/holdout support. Sparse history is displayed honestly.

A minimum of 90 need not return prospects. Yield depends on actual learned profiles, prospect features, coverage and filters. Scores are not stretched to guarantee counts. Similarity may also be common among people who will not buy. Marketing effectiveness requires independent outcome validation.

Before a demonstration:

1. Confirm exact code, source, catalog, profile and generation identities and actual full-5M certification.
2. Choose exact supported products and prepare verified similarity intelligence.
3. Explain the score mode, index meaning, and strict filters.
4. Show eligible, valid, qualified, selected and exportable counts separately.
5. Reopen immutable results; show the retained probability mode with its real limitations.
6. Export through an available governed channel profile and explain contact exclusions.
7. Present measured timings and actual scenario qualification, including blocked cases.
8. Label an illustrative phone/headphone fixture separately from canonical data.

Future work: prospective campaign outcomes for conversion/lift evaluation; richer features available at prospect time; a new score-method version if evidence supports improvement; ongoing source-distribution monitoring. These are not claimed completed by this prompt pack.
